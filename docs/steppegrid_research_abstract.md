# Research Abstract

Rural electricity planning requires models that connect local demand and variable renewable resources to system reliability and cost at an operational time scale. SteppeGrid is a reproducible platform for hourly wind–solar–battery microgrid simulation and discrete technology sizing across seven Kazakhstan settlements. Registered annual, monthly, or hourly electricity demand is represented as an 8,760-hour load trace and aligned with cached ERA5 weather. Turbine power curves, hub-height wind adjustment, photovoltaic and inverter models, and constrained battery state-of-charge dispatch produce hourly served load, unmet electricity, and curtailment. A staged discrete optimizer selects supported equipment combinations for 95% and 99% annual energy served targets, and lifecycle calculations report capital cost, net present cost, equivalent annual cost, and cost per served kilowatt-hour.

The frozen Rodina Benchmark shows a strong reliability–cost tradeoff: the selected 95% system has $49.4M net present cost, while the 99% system reaches $105.8M. Fourteen standardized Planner V2 scenarios provide a controlled cross-site comparison: within the five-site proxy cohort, Kegen has the highest PV specific yield, Togyzkuduk the highest representative wind capacity factor, and Sai-Otes the largest modeled 95%→99% NPC escalation. Shamshi Kaldayakova in Aktobe Region is the featured `MY VILLAGE` case. Its current 0.50 GWh/year dataset and standardized 95% design yield 95.83% annual energy served with 200 kW wind, 347.8 kWdc solar, 1.03 MWh storage, and $2.64M net present cost. SteppeGrid preserves source metadata, hashes, scenario snapshots, and audit artifacts while presenting the planning outputs through an interactive deployment-ready application.
