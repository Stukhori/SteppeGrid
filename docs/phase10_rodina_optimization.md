# Phase 10: efficient discrete Rodina optimization

## Scope and frozen baseline

This planning benchmark sizes catalog equipment against Rodina's reconstructed 8.02 GWh annual
load. It inherits the frozen 8,760-hour 2025 ERA5/UTC+05:00 Phase 9 data, the ERA5-derived shear
exponent 0.2317610498, and all Phase 8 physical models. Hourly demand is reconstructed rather
than measured; the source's printed 7.72 GWh remains explicitly inconsistent with its 8.02 GWh
monthly-row sum.

Only turbine model/count, fixed PV block type/count, and battery model/count vary. Hub heights
(10.7/9/30 m), PV tilt (51.302445 degrees), azimuth (180 degrees clockwise from north), battery
dispatch, and minimum initial SOC are fixed. The targets are 95% and 99% annual energy served;
energy-based LPSP is annual unmet energy divided by annual load.

“Optimal” below means lowest reference NPC among candidates generated by this declared staged
method, under one ERA5 year, a stated reconstructed load shape, deterministic dispatch, fixed
physical assumptions, and reference economics. It does not mean a construction recommendation
or a proven global optimum over every possible integer combination.

## Search method and bounds

One 8,760-hour trace is precomputed for each of three turbines and six Phase 9 PV blocks. Integer
counts scale those traces. Count ceilings equal three times annual load divided by each unit's
actual Phase 9 yield: wind maxima are 23,978 Skystream, 10,740 SD6, and 1,099 Bergey units; PV
maxima are 347/348 SMA blocks and 173 Fronius blocks depending on module. The theoretical full
Cartesian space is 505,169,460 combinations.

The implemented deterministic staged search uses wind-energy-share rays 0, 0.2, ..., 1.0,
constructs the annual-energy threshold directly, and adaptively brackets/bisects increasing integer
counts. It rejects generation below `target * annual load` before battery dispatch. Battery counts
start at 0-4 and exact minimum count is found by ordered monotonic search; dispatch results are
cached by renewable portfolio, load shape, product, and count. Zero storage has one canonical key.

All initial optima touched four batteries. Only those eight mode/target regions were audited at six
batteries and a four-times-load PV ceiling. The 99% regions that then selected six batteries were
checked selectively through eight. The final 95% solutions use four batteries inside a checked
0-6 range. The final 99% solutions use six inside a checked 0-8 range. Selected PV and wind counts
are below their audited maxima. No whole-space 0-8 rerun was performed.

## Computational results

The final run generated 5,444 unique renewable traces and 705 feasible staged candidates. The
conservative product-specific physical dominance rule retained all 705 because none was no worse
simultaneously in equipment quantities, every profile's reliability, and every profile's curtailment.
The original reference-economic robust cost/reliability filter retained 12 points; scale-aware
reranking reduces that compact frontier to 8 points. The run made 10,757 unique no-storage and
24,290 unique battery simulations. Caching avoided 49,727 repeated evaluations and ordered search
avoided another 77,947 count evaluations. Trace precomputation took 29.75 seconds, no-storage
screening 101.23 seconds, battery dispatch 372.04 seconds, other search orchestration 12.95 seconds,
economics 0.03 seconds, and total runtime 516.19 seconds. The annual-energy condition removes
25,496,910 combinations from the theoretical space before dispatch at the lowest primary target.
The final scale/reliability refinement reused all 705 saved candidates, introduced no candidate
search, and took 75.65 seconds. It replayed 513 already-declared robust candidate/profile cases
only because the original compact CSV did not retain every profile's duration metrics.

## Scale-aware reference economic assumptions

All monetary results use 2022 real USD, a 25-year horizon, 3% real discount rate, like-for-like
replacement at constant real cost, and zero residual value. These are reference planning economics,
not Kazakhstan contractor quotations.

| Technology | CAPEX | Fixed O&M | Life | Boundary/source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Distributed wind | $8,425/kW | $39/kW-year | 25 y | NREL 20-kW-class installed turbine+BOS benchmark |
| Commercial PV (<=5 MWac) | $1,990/kWdc | $21/kWdc-year | 25 y | NREL installed commercial PV including inverter/BOS |
| Utility-scale PV (>5 MWac) | $1,430/kWac | $24/kWac-year | 25 y | NREL installed utility PV including grid connection |
| Lithium-ion storage | $482/kWh | 2.5% CAPEX/year | 15 y | NREL installed four-hour technology-class proxy |

The financial-method reference is DOE/FEMP's
[2024 Annual Supplement to NIST Handbook 135](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/articles/annual-supplement-nist-handbook-135).
Technology-cost sources are NREL's [2022 Cost of Wind Energy](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy24osti/88335.pdf),
[2024 ATB Commercial PV](https://atb.nrel.gov/electricity/2024/commercial_pv),
[2024 ATB Utility-Scale PV](https://atb.nrel.gov/electricity/2024/utility-scale_pv), and
[2023 Battery Storage Cost Update](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy23osti/85332.pdf). The battery
proxy is not Tesla or Saft pricing, and the wind value is not a Bergey quote. Tesla and Saft remain
physically distinct while sharing the monetary proxy. PV classification is discrete: installed AC
nameplate at or below 5 MWac uses the commercial class; above 5 MWac uses the utility class and
its AC-denominated basis. The threshold follows the utility-scale source page's discussion of EIA
installations greater than 5 MWac; no unsupported interpolation is used. Wind economics still
extrapolate a distributed-wind $/kW reference to many identical units and do not represent a
multi-turbine quote, project-scale economies, wakes/layout, or site-specific civil works.

NPC equals initial CAPEX plus discounted fixed O&M and replacements at integer multiples of
component lifetime before year 25. Equivalent annual cost is `NPC * CRF`. Cost per served kWh
uses equivalent annual cost divided by annual load actually served.

## Robust results and cross-profile uncertainty

The robust 95% result is the residential-specific 95% design: 131 Bergey turbines, 83
Trina450/Fronius blocks, and four Tesla units. Its binding served fraction is 95.038% residential.
Its served fractions are 95.353% flat, 95.038% residential, and 95.781% community. Initial CAPEX is $36.52M, NPC
$49.38M, and equivalent annual cost $2.836M. The module choice changed from Trina 460 to Trina
450 during scale-aware reranking; wind, PV-block count, and storage count did not change.

The robust 99% result is the residential-specific 99% design: 319 Bergey turbines, 202
Trina450/Fronius blocks, and six Tesla units. Its served fractions are 99.006% flat, 99.003%
residential, and 99.132% community; residential is binding. Initial CAPEX is $81.96M, NPC
$105.79M, and equivalent annual cost $6.075M. Its physical configuration did not change.

Every selected PV system exceeds 5 MWac and therefore uses the utility-scale class. Wind uses the
distributed-wind reference and storage uses the common lithium-ion reference in every selected case.

## Energy reliability and illustrative duration constraint

The primary 95% and 99% constraints refer to annual load energy served, not uptime or the fraction
of outage-free hours. Energy-based LPSP is `unmet energy / annual load`. Loss-of-load hours count
hours with unmet energy above 1e-6 kWh. Longest deficit is the longest consecutive run of such
hours; maximum hourly deficit is the largest unmet amount in one hourly timestep.

The energy-only robust 95% design still has 532 binding-profile loss-of-load hours and a 41-hour
continuous deficit. The robust 99% design has 109 loss-of-load hours and a 16-hour binding-profile
run; across all profiles its longest run is 34 hours. Thus “99% annual load energy served” is not
“99% uptime” or outage-free operation.

One deliberately simple sensitivity imposes `longest deficit <= 24 hours` in addition to the energy
target. This is an illustrative research planning constraint, not a Kazakhstan or government
reliability standard. No loss-of-load-hour ceiling or threshold grid is imposed.

For the robust duration-constrained design, served fractions are 99.161% flat, 99.001%
residential, and 99.343% community; longest runs are 16, 15, and 22 hours respectively. The lack
of a feasible community-specific case means none of the saved community-mode candidates meets its
energy target and the 24-hour limit; it is not a claim of universal physical infeasibility.

Cross-validation shows material load-shape fragility. The community-specific 95% design serves
only 90.61% of flat and 89.26% of residential demand. Its 99% counterpart serves 98.88% flat and
98.87% residential. Profile-specific labels must therefore not be read as robust results.

Within the declared robust candidate set, the least-cost 95% PV+storage family candidate has NPC
$60.29M, versus $49.38M for wind+PV+storage. No PV+storage candidate reached robust 99% in the
declared staged set. This is conditional evidence, not universal technology-family superiority.

## Pareto interpretation and limitations

Scale-aware reranking reduces the compact robust cost/reliability frontier from 12 to 8 points and
still shows a sharp cost and curtailment increase from
95% to 99%. A 99.9% sensitivity is technically worth studying only after demand measurements,
multi-year weather, and missing physical loss mechanisms are improved; it is not run here.

Demand is reconstructed. Weather is one ERA5 year. Wind shear is ERA5-derived, not measured;
density, wakes, icing, turbulence, and availability are absent. PV omits shading, snow, and soiling
and uses simplified temperature and constant inverter efficiency. PV blocks are not construction
string designs. Batteries omit degradation and thermal behavior and use fixed dispatch. Economics
are international/U.S. reference values, not local bids. No unsupported derating factors were added,
and Shamshi demand was neither estimated nor optimized.
